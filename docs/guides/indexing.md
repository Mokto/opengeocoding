## Overture address index

`build_index` builds a Tantivy index and house-number tables from a local [Overture Maps](https://docs.overturemaps.org/) release, without Nominatim. The service loads that index (`src/index/`) and it is its only supported lookup backend. The [coverage limitations](../evaluation/coverage.md#house-number-coverage) and [Reverse geocoding](api.md#reverse-geocoding) apply.

`build_index job` (see the [deployment guide](deployment.md#deploy-on-kubernetes)) fetches all of the sources below itself. To build by hand, download the `division` and `division_area` (divisions theme), `segment` (transportation) and `address` (addresses) parquet files of one release into same-named directories, about 100 GB in total. This example requires the AWS CLI and the [local build prerequisites](development.md#build-and-test):

```sh
aws s3 sync --no-sign-request \
  s3://overturemaps-us-west-2/release/2026-09-23.1/theme=divisions/type=division/ overture/division/
aws s3 sync --no-sign-request \
  s3://overturemaps-us-west-2/release/2026-09-23.1/theme=divisions/type=division_area/ overture/division_area/
aws s3 sync --no-sign-request \
  s3://overturemaps-us-west-2/release/2026-09-23.1/theme=transportation/type=segment/ overture/segment/
aws s3 sync --no-sign-request \
  s3://overturemaps-us-west-2/release/2026-09-23.1/theme=addresses/type=address/ overture/address/
cargo build --locked --release --bin build_index
target/release/build_index overture out/
```

Optionally download `place` (places theme, `theme=places/type=place`, about 11 GB) into `overture/place/`. After provisioning additional sources, pass their paths to the builder:

```sh
target/release/build_index overture out/ --openaddresses openaddresses/ --osm osm/ \
  --geonames-postal GB_full.txt --geonames-postal allCountries.txt
```

Overture's address theme covers about 40 countries. The optional sources fill the rest:

- `--osm DIR`: every `*.osm.pbf` in the directory. Addressed nodes, addressed ways and multipolygon relations at their first node, `addr:place` addresses (numbered within the place), and `addr:interpolation` ways (the numbers between their addressed nodes, spaced along the way; Argentina's block ranges alone give about 45 million); the country is that of the containing place. A way or relation not tagged as a `building` or `building:part` contributes a house number only when every outer vertex is present and within 300 m of its first vertex, using the existing house-copy merge scale as a conservative site-size heuristic. Larger or incomplete sites still contribute their street, postcode and postal-town metadata, not a building point; separately addressed nodes and explicitly tagged buildings remain eligible. Files must be sorted by type then id, as [Geofabrik](https://download.geofabrik.de/) extracts are. Pick the extracts for countries without Overture addresses from `download.geofabrik.de/index-v1-nogeom.json` (`iso3166-1:alpha2`); about 150 extracts and 30 GB in October 2026. Add extracts where Overture's coverage has holes: in October 2026 it had no addresses for Bavaria or the city of Milan, so `germany/bayern` and `italy` were added (3 GB). Extracts overlap at borders and with Overture; duplicate house numbers collapse. Landmarks (see [Address suggestions](api.md#address-suggestions)) are read from these extracts too.
- `--osm-landmarks DIR`: every `*.osm.pbf` in the directory, read for landmarks only, never for addresses, whose answers would change in countries whose addresses come from Overture. The chart's `osmLandmarks` lists the 27 extracts of the countries `osm` leaves out (Western and Central Europe, North America, Australia and others; 42 GB in October 2026).
- `place/` in the Overture release directory, when present (`build_index job` always fetches it): Overture's places theme (points of interest from Meta, Microsoft and others under CDLA-Permissive-2.0, Foursquare under Apache-2.0, AllThePlaces under CC0-1.0). A place whose freeform address is only a house number and a street (`553 Sư Vạn Hạnh`, `44 Ryneveld Street`; not `Shop 5, Mall Road`, `548/11 Rajajipuram` or `Calle Mayor 5`) gives that house at the place's point, when the street is a road that Overture's segments name in the same place and 0.1° cell, and only in the United Kingdom and Vietnam in this first rollout, where fewer than 10,000 Overture address rows with a street fall in that country (counted by the place that holds them, so Réunion's BAN addresses, which Overture files under France, cover Réunion). The place's postcode and locality are not read: whoever listed the business typed them. Its houses rank last among duplicates, and they neither move a street's point nor add to its weight, so a road with one listed business keeps its segments' middle and its rank among same-named roads. In the October 2026 build it added 836,488 houses in those two countries and no street; see [House-number coverage](../evaluation/coverage.md#house-number-coverage).
- `--openaddresses DIR`: every `*.geojson.gz` in the directory, one OpenAddresses batch job output each, named `<cc>_<source path>-<layer>.geojson.gz` (the country comes from the prefix). List address layers with `batch.openaddresses.io/api/data` and download `v2.openaddresses.io/batch-prod/job/<job>/source.geojson.gz` for the sources Overture doesn't list (about 2,700 files, 13 GB in October 2026). Many overlap Overture's data; identical house numbers on the same street collapse (the copy from the best-ranked source is kept, Overture before OpenAddresses before OSM before Overture's places, then the one with the smallest coordinates; it takes the postcode of the first duplicate that has one when it has none, so a source without postcodes never strips one), and in a sample 19% of US and 60% of Mexican OpenAddresses addresses were on a street the Overture-only index had without that number. Each file needs its source definition beside it as `<cc>_<source path>-<layer>.source.json`: the JSON at the job's `source` URL from `batch.openaddresses.io/api/job/<job>` (for a deleted source, its last version in the `openaddresses/openaddresses` git history; `build_index job` doesn't recover those, so it reads them only with `--openaddresses-any-licence`, and in October 2026 that left out about 400 files). Only files whose layer licence is recognised as public domain, open with at most attribution, or ODbL are read; a file with no definition, no stated licence, an unrecognised one, another share-alike licence or a ban on sale or commercial use is skipped. The classification is a conservative text match, not legal review. A file whose definition states no licence is read anyway when `src/bin/build_index/openaddresses_reviewed.tsv` lists it: those entries were checked against the publisher's own terms (statewide files for 12 US states and DC, a few US cities and counties, and sources in Canada, Austria, Germany, Hong Kong and Argentina; the file also records the sources read and refused, with the reason). The list never overrides a stated ban on sale or commercial use, or a share-alike licence, and each entry's terms and credit line go to `ATTRIBUTION.txt`. `--openaddresses-any-licence` reads every file. Each file's licence and whether it was read go to `out/openaddresses.tsv`.
- `--korea-shops ZIP`: the Small Enterprise and Market Service's [상가(상권)정보](https://www.data.go.kr/data/15083033/fileData.do), a quarterly zip of UTF-8 CSVs with Korean road and land-lot addresses and WGS84 coordinates. The publisher states `이용허락범위 제한 없음` (no restriction on reuse); this is a separate source from the restricted juso.go.kr/OpenAddresses layers, which remain excluded. Each business supplies both address forms, including mountain lots (`산`); duplicate houses merge, with all existing address sources preferred over these business points. Existing street centroids and importance are preserved; supplementary-only streets get IDs after primary streets to preserve existing candidate tie order. Names, contacts, industry codes and floor/unit fields are not indexed. A business point can lie inside a campus rather than at its entrance; shop coverage is not residential coverage. The June 2026 file contains 2.77 million businesses in about 1.12 million buildings, not a national register of all buildings. The chart pins the attachment ID and SHA-256 in `index.sources.koreaShops`; omit that key to exclude it. The build Job checks the publisher's terms before the first download, verifies the checksum, then seals the original zip and provenance in `sources/korea-shops/<sha256>`, reused without upstream requests. A bounding box outside Korea skips this source. Manual builds accept the downloaded zip directly with `--korea-shops shops.zip`; verify its provenance and checksum yourself. A new release requires reviewing its terms, updating both pins and comparing the resulting index, not changing `snapshot` alone.
- Ryhti's Swedish-only addresses (`index.sources.ryhti: true`; `build_index job` only): OpenAddresses' `fi/countrywide-fi` and `fi/countrywide-sv` layers cut the house number off the Finnish address text, so the addresses Syke's Ryhti register names only in Swedish (about 35,000 numbered ones in October 2026, most of Åland's and some of Ostrobothnia's) come without a number. The build Job asks Ryhti's WFS for those rows in one request (CC BY 4.0, "Lähde: Syke Ryhti"; see [the Finnish corpus](../evaluation/registry/fi.md)), writes them as the OpenAddresses layer `fi_ryhti-swedish.geojson.gz` with its `.source.json`, seals them under `sources/<snapshot>/ryhti/` and passes that directory as a second `--openaddresses`; they merge with the other sources' houses like any OpenAddresses layer. A bounding box outside Finland skips it. Drop it once those layers read Ryhti's number fields. Manual builds can pass such a directory the same way.
- INEGI's establishment directory, DENUE (`index.sources.denue.before`; `build_index job` only): the build Job downloads the 33 state zips of INEGI's current edition (`www.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/masiva/denue/denue_<state>_csv.zip`, about 575 MB; INEGI serves no earlier one, so it is fetched under `snapshot`) and writes one OpenAddresses layer, `mx_denue-establishments`, with its source definition: each fixed establishment that entered the directory before `before` and has a named street that is not a highway and an exterior number of one to five digits (a letter joined, "5B"), its street type before the name ("CALLE UNIÓN"), postcode and locality, written once per address and point. The edition, read from the zips' metadata, is named in the credit line; INEGI's *Términos de Libre Uso* allow commercial use with that credit. Names, activities and contact details are not read. The layer ranks as a business source, after every address source's copy of the same house. 05_2026 gives 2.74 million points from 6.14 million establishments. The Mexico corpus samples establishments that entered DENUE from `DENUE_SAMPLED_FROM` (`src/eval.rs`), which `tests/chart.rs` keeps equal to `before`; moving one means sampling that corpus again. A bounding box outside Mexico skips this source. Manual builds read such a layer, with its source definition, through `--openaddresses`.
- Uruguay's doors with a letter (`index.sources.dgu: true`; `build_index job` only): OpenAddresses' `uy/*/regionwide` layers read IDE Uruguay's address base, Direcciones Geográficas del Uruguay, but keep only `num_puerta`, so a door "109 BIS" or "283A" comes as the plain number. The build Job downloads the base's 19 department CSVs from the Catálogo de Datos Abiertos (about 180 MB, Windows-1252; the catalogue serves only the latest monthly files, so they are fetched under `snapshot`) and writes the doors with one capital letter ("283A") or `BIS` ("109 BIS") on a named street as the OpenAddresses layer `uy_dgu-letters`, with postcode and locality (Licencia de Datos Abiertos – Uruguay, credited to IDE Uruguay and the Correo Uruguayo; see [the Uruguay corpus](../evaluation/registry/uy.md)), sealed under `sources/<snapshot>/dgu/` and passed as another `--openaddresses`. The files of 2026-10-01 give 22,659 doors. A bounding box outside Uruguay skips it. Drop it once those layers join `letra_puerta`. Manual builds can pass such a directory the same way.
- `--bbox MINLON,MINLAT,MAXLON,MAXLAT`: index only streets, addresses and house numbers inside the box. Places from the input are kept as they are.
- `--geonames-postal FILE`, repeatable: GeoNames postal-code dumps (`download.geonames.org/export/zip/`, CC-BY-4.0). `allCountries` has only outward codes for GB and 3-character codes for CA; `GB_full`, `CA_full` and `NL_full` hold full codes. A postcode is added only when the address data and earlier files lack it.
- `--geonames-places FILE [--geonames-admin1 FILE]`: GeoNames populated places and `admin1CodesASCII.txt` (`download.geonames.org/export/dump/`, CC-BY-4.0). `build_index job` passes the `P` (populated place) rows of `allCountries.zip`, about 5.2 million, which it keeps as `populated.txt` under `sources/<snapshot>/geonames-populated/`; `cities500.txt`, a subset, works too. A populated place is added (as a locality, or a neighborhood for `PPLX`; historical, abandoned, destroyed and religious ones are skipped) only when no place of the same country and folded name, under any of its names, lies within 10 km, so it fills a gap and leaves Overture's places as they are. A place answers to its name and ASCII name only: GeoNames' other names include labels like "Huy (Tihange)" and the names of the place's district, which then answered queries for other places. Its parent is the place that holds it (as for addresses); with none, the Overture region named by its admin1 name. Street and address assignment does not see these places. The gold corpus's cities were picked from GeoNames, so its gain from this source overstates the gain for places GeoNames lacks.

  A place below `cities500`'s bar (500 people or fewer, unless it is a capital or the seat of a first- to fourth-order division) is held to more: it is added only when nothing else of its country has its name, neither a place of the index (of any kind, anywhere in the country: a country, region or county too) nor another GeoNames row more than 10 km away; of rows of one name within 10 km of each other, the first is kept. It never adds its names to a place the index has, and a section of a populated place (`PPLX`) of that size is skipped. Such places are written with class `geonames_small` and reach only two lookups: suggestions, after every other locality of their name, and a search for exactly `<name>, <country>` that nothing below the country answers otherwise (the resolver's last step, which also answers a county named like the query with the locality of that name inside it, takes "X" for "X Village" or "X Hamlet" and back, and answers nothing when two localities of the name lie 10 km or more apart).

  The same step reads `alternates.fst`: GeoNames' other names of a row (`alternatenames`), in the Latin script and without digits or parentheses, mapped to the place the row added or matched ("Gahnpa" is Ganta, "Cape Palmas" Harper). A name goes in only when no place of the index in that country has it and no GeoNames row more than 10 km away has it as a name or another name; in October 2026 that is 11 MB. Nothing else reads these names: they include labels and old names that misled matching when places answered to them. Other searches, ReverseGeocode, ResolveRegion and the locality that names a street do not see them. On the October 2026 world sources this adds about 1.54 million places (1.56 million GeoNames places in all, against 23,000 from `cities500`), 151 MB of `places.tsv` and 0.2 GB of index (17.13 to 17.32 GB). In one pair of back-to-back world builds on an 18-core laptop the build took 471 s instead of 444 s and peaked at a 16.1 GB memory footprint instead of 15.9 GB; the service loads 6.26 million places instead of 4.72 million, in 6.4 s instead of 5.2 s, with a 4.9 GB physical footprint instead of 4.3 GB. These are single measurements. Without these limits, all 5.2 million rows added 3.2 million places: tiny namesakes then answered address fallbacks ("Philippines" and "South Africa" are GeoNames villages, "Via Nazionale Borgata Ravotti" an Italian one), took the place of better partial matches, and named points for reverse lookups, often as spelling variants of the Overture village beside them ("Tabonibara Village"). Many Wikidata settlements were imported from GeoNames, so the town-level corpus (`tests/gold.towns.jsonl`) gains from this partly because both carry the same records: 378 of the 457 too-coarse town cases with a same-named GeoNames place within 5 km had it within 1 km of Wikidata's point. That gain is not independent evidence of accuracy.

  `build_index job` always reads it; `index.sources` has no switch for it, nor for OpenAddresses (whose licence check always applies).

The writer leaves one Tantivy segment per arena flush (84 for a world index) and every query looks its terms up in each, so the build commits places, streets and postcodes apart and ends by merging them into one segment, places first, then postcodes, then streets: a lookup reads a word's postings only up to the end of its kind's documents, and a place's words also name millions of streets. The street segments are merged in four parallel groups first; the whole merge took about 70 seconds for a world index, against 40 seconds for the four segments earlier builds kept, which queried about 13% slower. Each place and street also indexes its name words, and each street its postal city's words, behind its country (`cnames`, `ccity`): tantivy walks a word's skip list one block at a time, so a lookup in one country reads that country's postings of a common word ("st", "rue") rather than every country's.

The output holds places, streets (one per normalized name per place and 0.1° cell, merging road names with address streets) and postcodes in Tantivy, plus house numbers with coordinates in sharded FSTs. The cell keeps same-named streets of one large locality apart (Port Chalmers' and central Dunedin's Beach Street); a street crossing a cell edge is indexed in parts, and a lookup answers the middle of the parts. Addresses without a street (Overture's Polish, Czech, Slovak and Baltic village addresses, OSM `addr:place`) are numbered on a street named after their village.

Source postal towns are also stored separately from settlement ancestry, with their source field (`postal_city`, `address_levels`, OpenAddresses `city`/`district` or OSM `addr:city`). Within a street's assigned locality, the house's own postcode scopes this evidence; without a source postcode, only the exact house number can lend a town. Conflicting names within a scope do not corroborate it. This lets a house name its postal town even when that name is also a county, without allowing the county alone to vouch for the address or replacing its settlement/admin fields. Existing requested-city display conventions remain unchanged. A conflicting supplied postcode can be recovered on the raw house lookup only with an independently matching source postal town, a recognized country and consistent stated region. The code must be indexed in that country or differ from the house's own numeric postcode by one substitution. No matching street may hold the requested postcode (including a compatible postcode area), and every indexed occurrence of that street and house number in the country must lie within 100 m of the candidate; a saturated 1,000-street search declines recovery. Such an answer retains the source postcode and has `goodMatch=false`. Unknown nonnumeric codes, ambiguous houses and same-street postcode conflicts remain rejected.

An administrative ancestor can also corroborate a house's town when the existing index identifies it and a settlement by the same nonempty Wikidata item, country and ISO region. A shared name alone does not qualify. This uses existing source identity, not a postal-town alias or a new import; settlement/admin fields remain unchanged. A stated postcode still needs the house's own compatible postcode on this path, and country/state conflicts remain constraints. See [postal-town recovery](../notes/postal-town-recovery.md) for the scope and evaluation.

Names are folded by one analyzer at build and query time: Unicode tokens, lowercased, ASCII-folded, then street-name abbreviations canonicalized (`src/index/canon.rs`: `Avenue`/`Ave`/`Avenida`/`Av.` → `av`, `Straße` and compounds → `str`, `Calle`/`C/` → `cl`, `Drottninggatan` → `drottningg` like `Drottningg.`, and so on for English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Dutch, Nordic, Hungarian, Italian, Turkish, Polish, Russian and Ukrainian; Japanese chōme and Taiwanese section numerals read as digits, so `芝公園四丁目` and `芝公園4丁目` match). A query must use the same analyzer, including when correcting typos against the canonical vocabulary.

For house queries, SearchAddress also expands French `R.` to `Rue`, Dutch compound `ln.` to `laan`, and Norwegian compound `vn.` to `veien` or `vegen` (including before further road-name words) at lookup time, only after an exact street miss, with a recognized country and a resolved town or indexed postcode. Undotted compound abbreviations and names with multiple supported dotted compound abbreviations are not expanded. It checks all alternatives and declines the expansion when they identify different street documents. The same guarded fallback expands dotted German `str.` compounds with stems of at least two letters in Switzerland, Germany and Austria, and repairs one-edit errors in a final compound `strasse` suffix before the analyzer shortens it to `str`. In South Africa it also tries a final `Straat` as `Street` and vice versa. These repairs still require an existing exact house number and decline a conflicting house postcode. The resolver corroborates these spellings under the same country constraint. This does not merge full road names globally, change the stored index or add abbreviation completion to SuggestAddress.

Streets and addresses are assigned to the containing locality polygon, else a locality point within 5 km, else the local admin area or county, constrained by the source country or containing administration. An address none of these places holds is kept without a place: with its source's country, or, for OSM, which names none, the country of the region, dependency or country polygon that holds it (an OSM address no such polygon holds is dropped). Such a house is answered with the region (else the dependency or country) of the territorial polygon that holds it, and with the postal town its address names as its city. Direct locality-to-region parents that conflict with a unique same-country containing region are reconciled at import; [assignment diagnosis and validation](../notes/administrative-assignment.md) describe the rules and remaining limitations. Each polygon also keeps a grid over its bounding box, about one cell per edge, whose cells away from every edge are marked inside or outside, so most points are placed without reading an edge; the polygons whose boxes hold a point are found in nested grids of 0.2° to 51.2° cells. House numbers are spilled to `out/.numbers-spill` (about 7 GB for the world) and streets' postal towns to `out/.towns-spill` during the build, and the administrative polygons used to assign places (about 5 GB) are written to `out/.polygons` and memory-mapped; all three are removed afterwards. The builder reads available memory at startup (cgroup limit and usage, capped by the host's `MemAvailable`; `vm_stat` on macOS) and sizes the index writer and the number sort from the remaining headroom. On 2026-10-27, with that headroom held to 12 GB, a world build of Overture 2026-09-23.1 with the chart's OSM and landmark extracts, OpenAddresses and GeoNames peaked at a 12.1 GB memory footprint on an 18-core laptop with 64 GB RAM, against 25.6 GB for the previous builder under the same limit, for 511 million house numbers and 39 million streets. The mapped polygons are not part of that footprint: as file pages, the OS keeps them cached while memory allows and reads them back from disk under pressure, so a container short of room for them in its page cache reads the sources more slowly instead of failing. Most of the rest is held while the sources are read: the street and segment tables and the places. Given more headroom, the number sort and the writer use up to about half of it. These are single measurements, not guarantees.

Known limitations: outside Overture's address countries, house numbers are only as complete as OSM and OpenAddresses there, with addresses of points of interest also read in the UK and Vietnam (sparse in the UK, India and much of Africa and Asia; see House-number coverage); a point of interest is placed where its listing puts it, which is not always its door; OSM ways and relations are placed at their first node, not a verified entrance; the footprint guard rejects house numbers on large untyped sites but trusts explicit building tags and does not identify a building within a shared campus; interpolated numbers assume even spacing; abbreviations outside that table (`MLK`, `CR 123` for County Road) still index as separate streets; house numbers are matched lowercased and without full-width forms or a trailing 号/號, otherwise exactly as written. Overture's divisions and transportation themes and OpenStreetMap are ODbL (attribution "© OpenStreetMap contributors", share-alike for a distributed derived database); OpenAddresses licenses vary per source and many state none; review them before distributing an index. Builds are reproducible: two builds of the same sources and builder produce the same documents, terms and house numbers, so a gold comparison of two indexes shows only the change under test. `build_index digest DIR [--dump FILE]` prints a checksum of each part (documents, term counts, house numbers with their postcodes) that ignores segment layout, so two builds can be checked with it; the files themselves still differ byte for byte, because tantivy names its segments at random. Order-dependent choices are made from content instead: a street shows its spelling that is not all capitals and sorts first, keeps the postcodes, postal cities and alternative names that sort first when it has more than 20, 5 or 64, and takes its centroid from sums in units of 2^-24 degree; a segment joins only an address street of its place's country; street ids follow the streets' key order. Lookups break ties between equally important candidates by id, then coordinates, not by document order, which depends on how many threads wrote the index.
