# Norway

[Evaluation guide](../evaluation.md) · [Source notice](../../tests/registry/no.NOTICE)

**Norway** (`no.seeds.jsonl`, `no.jsonl`): 714 road addresses and 357 damaged variants drawn directly from Kartverket's national Matrikkelen–Adresse CSV export of 2026-10-10 (© Kartverket, CC BY 4.0). The file contains 2,603,963 rows, including 2,569,425 road addresses. `sample-no` takes two different street names per municipality, ranked by seeded SHA-256 of the address UUID, from a pool of the smallest 100 hashes per municipality. All 357 Norwegian municipalities in the 15 counties are represented; Svalbard and Jan Mayen are excluded. This weights municipalities equally, not their populations or address counts. It includes 84 houses with letter suffixes and uses the registry's postal town, which need not be the municipality or nearest settlement.

```sh
unzip -p matrikkelen-adresse-2026-10-10.zip '*/matrikkelenAdresse.csv' > matrikkelen-adresse-4258-2026-10-10.csv
target/release/evaluate sample-no --kartverket matrikkelen-adresse-4258-2026-10-10.csv --seed no-2026-10 --out tests/registry/no.seeds.jsonl
target/release/evaluate build-gold --seeds tests/registry/no.seeds.jsonl --out tests/registry/no.jsonl --kartverket matrikkelen-adresse-4258-2026-10-10.csv
target/release/evaluate run --corpus tests/registry/no.jsonl --index out --out eval-results/no
```

Retain the [national EPSG:4258 archive](https://nedlasting.geonorge.no/geonorge/Basisdata/MatrikkelenAdresse/CSV/Basisdata_0000_Norge_4258_MatrikkelenAdresse_CSV.zip): its URL moves with each release. This archive is 151,708,361 bytes, SHA-256 `d2ba050754e71aac18ffd572538ee97d43e3b00c543ef567dfc8a421d99bef7c`; its extracted CSV is 812,385,818 bytes, SHA-256 `02b2bbc896a65d4510cf8159d26eed3d3f777c8059cc011699b1b4dd4383910d`. Every case records the CSV filename and checksum. `build-gold --kartverket` reads each cited UUID again and copies its house number, letter suffix, street, postcode and point, never a geocoder's answer. It rejects a projected-coordinate export rather than treating metres as latitude and longitude. EPSG:4258 (ETRS89) coordinates are used directly at the 150 m tolerance, not with a survey-grade datum transformation. Cadastral addresses without a road, rows with missing address components and invalid points are outside the sample. County numbers are tags, not inferred ISO region expectations, so these cases do not check ResolveRegion.

One of the two addresses in each municipality also gets an edit, balanced among the eleven kinds that apply: no country, no postcode, uppercase, lowercase, no commas, reversed parts, ASCII transliteration, a street or town typo, or a different sampled postcode or town from the same municipality. A variant retains the registry expectation and location's hold-out assignment. Consistent edits require goodMatch true; typos and contradictory parts accept either value. The source selects the cases independently of Overture and the service, but Norwegian index data can share this same upstream register, so a pass measures fidelity to the register, not independent truth or production accuracy.

On the same local full index as above, 706 of 714 base addresses and 331 of 357 variants passed; 130 of 131 held-out base addresses passed. There were no critical results, and no known-failure labels were added. The 34 failures remain visible and unlabelled pending diagnosis; many postcode-omission variants fall back to the postal town even when direct lookup finds the house. ReverseGeocode returned Norway for all 1,071 points. SuggestAddress found none of the 714 complete base-address strings and 24 variants. A same-binary repeat had zero result or class drift, though its one-request speed section flagged p50 increases of 20% for SuggestAddress and 14% for ReverseGeocode; these laptop timings are not latency guarantees. Comparing the existing 4,996-case suite with the expanded 6,067-case suite changed no shared results and passed the relative gate. Both standalone full runs failed the then-unresolved Rue de Rivoli must-pass case. Sampler output was byte-identical on a repeat; expectations and service behaviour were not changed.
