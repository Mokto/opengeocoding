# Switzerland

[Evaluation guide](../README.md) · [Source notice](../../../tests/registry/ch.NOTICE)

**Switzerland** (`ch.seeds.jsonl`, `ch.jsonl`): 520 addresses and 260 damaged variants from the official building-address directory (©swisstopo, its OGD terms), whose STAC item is dated 2026-10-16 and whose CSV asset was updated on 2026-10-15. The export contains 3,304,410 rows. `sample-ch` selects real, official, residential addresses, ranks their EGAIDs by seeded SHA-256, and takes 20 different municipality/street pairs in each of the 26 cantons from its smallest 500 hashes. The sample includes German, French, Italian and Romansh street names. Equal canton weighting is not population weighting; the eligibility filter is particularly restrictive in Valais (3,585 eligible rows) and Graubünden (7,010), so this is not a representative national sample.

```sh
unzip -p swisstopo-addresses-2056-2026-10-16.csv.zip '*.csv' > swisstopo-addresses-2056-2026-10-16.csv
target/release/evaluate sample-ch --swisstopo swisstopo-addresses-2056-2026-10-16.csv --seed ch-2026-10 --out tests/registry/ch.seeds.jsonl
target/release/evaluate build-gold --seeds tests/registry/ch.seeds.jsonl --out tests/registry/ch.jsonl --swisstopo swisstopo-addresses-2056-2026-10-16.csv
target/release/evaluate run --corpus tests/registry/ch.jsonl --index out --out eval-results/ch
```

Retain the [national Swiss CSV archive](https://data.geo.admin.ch/ch.swisstopo.amtliches-gebaeudeadressverzeichnis/amtliches-gebaeudeadressverzeichnis_ch/amtliches-gebaeudeadressverzeichnis_ch_2056.csv.zip), whose URL moves with releases. The ZIP is 143,331,394 bytes, SHA-256 `0965c7271d3bc90ea6c6d46c68738e66dc860459847988ff7e697eb77eb169e8` (also the STAC asset's SHA-256); the CSV is 468,469,954 bytes, SHA-256 `c8d6d5f8777656bcbc364ec867a8b7641d370e90cc97083021c1c4441f6bba82`. Each case records the CSV filename and checksum. `build-gold --swisstopo` re-reads its EGAID and copies the house number, street and postcode. The LV95/EPSG:2056 coordinates are converted by swisstopo's official [REFRAME service](https://geodesy.geo.admin.ch/reframe/lv95towgs84), once per location, sequentially with 100 ms between requests. This step needs network access; it is coordinate conversion, not address geocoding. The recorded conversion uses altitude zero because the CSV is two-dimensional, and the cases assert no height. Points are rounded to six decimal places and scored within 150 m. Planned, unofficial, non-residential and invalid-coordinate rows are outside the frame. The source's canton abbreviation supplies the `CH-XX` expectation, so SearchAddress, ReverseGeocode and ResolveRegion all check the canton.

Half of the locations get one of the same eleven edits as Norway, balanced among applicable kinds; the wrong postcode or town is another sampled one in that canton. Variants keep the same point, canton and location key. Consistent edits require goodMatch true; typos or conflicting components accept either value. No service response selects a case or supplies an expectation. Registry and index addresses can share upstream data, so agreement is fidelity to the register, not independent truth or production accuracy.

On the local full index, 504 of 520 base cases and 246 of 260 variants passed; 86 of 90 held-out base cases passed. All 30 failures remain visible and unlabelled: 19 wrong canton, five too coarse, three near misses, two confidence mismatches and one abstention. There were no critical results. Several wrong-canton houses are within 2 m of their source point but filed under a neighbouring canton (notably Schaffhausen under Zurich). ReverseGeocode passed 507/520 base cases and ResolveRegion 513/520; SuggestAddress found no complete base address and 18/260 variants. A Swiss same-binary repeat had zero result or class drift. The relative full-suite comparison passed, with all 6,067 shared classes unchanged, but repeated the already documented French La Torlière/La Petite Torlière wrong-answer drift and flagged SuggestAddress p90 +13% and p99 +14% in its one-request speed section. Both full runs failed the then-unresolved Rue de Rivoli must-pass case. Sampler and full corpus regeneration (including a fresh REFRAME conversion) were byte-identical; re-scoring the expanded 6,847-case run changed no classes. These timings are not a clean performance comparison, and no service behaviour or expectations were relaxed.

Each case's `places`, the names ReverseGeocode's answer must include one of, are `ZIP_LABEL`'s locality and `COM_NAME`, each also without the canton that tells namesakes apart ("Buchs AG" is also Buchs).

## Reverse offset boundary control

The source point of EGAID `101742758` (Alte Hägglingerstrasse 41b, Dottikon) is `[47.385813, 8.243970]`. Its deterministic 25 m offset is `[47.3860377, 8.2439816]`, across the municipal boundary in Hägglingen. The official capture independently identifies EGAID `102870001` as Talackerstrasse **15b.1**, postcode **5607**, municipality **Hägglingen**, canton **AG**. Its LV95 point `[2660812.505, 1248665.399]` converts through REFRAME to `[47.386013, 8.244004]`, about 3 m from the offset query. Converting the query back through REFRAME and scanning all real, official capture rows independently gives 15b.1 as the nearest point (3.24 m); the next is 15b at 9.51 m. The sampler records it as `reverse_offset_ref`; regeneration reads its number, postcode, place and point from that row. The offset expectation requires this house within 10 m of its official point, not any house in Hägglingen. The original 41b forward, suggestion and at-point reverse expectations remain unchanged.

This neighbouring address is real and official but **non-residential**. Explicit source-ID lookup can read it; the sampler still selects residential addresses only, with its other eligibility filters unchanged. The generated offset has its own source reference, capture checksum, licence and conversion provenance. It is not a service-derived expected answer or a general relaxation of border checks.

The municipality assignment uses the containing `localadmin` polygon to constrain nearby settlement points. An unclassed locality polygon wider than that municipality (Ticino's *circoli*) gives way to it; a classed city spanning boroughs does not. Where no settlement point in the municipality is close enough, the municipality itself names the house. This uses mapped boundaries, not a claim of surveyed boundary precision.
