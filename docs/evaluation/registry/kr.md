# South Korea, school-sampled

[Evaluation guide](../README.md) · [Source notice](../../../tests/registry/kr.NOTICE)

**South Korea, school-sampled** (`kr.seeds.jsonl`, `kr.jsonl`): 207 schools and 414 paired road-name/land-lot queries, drawn directly from the official [national school-location dataset](https://www.data.go.kr/data/15021148/standard.do), provided by 한국교육시설안전원 through data.go.kr. This is a targeted sample from a public school directory, including privately operated schools, **not a sample of the national address register or an estimate of residential-address accuracy**. The national [entrance-coordinate register](https://www.data.go.kr/data/15050410/fileData.do) requires an application, identity verification and approval; this corpus does not use it or a commercial geocoder.

The snapshot captured on 2026-10-17 contains 12,004 operating schools, all with reference date 2026-10-01 and coordinates. It has 16 education-authority codes, including the combined 전남광주통합특별시 authority. Eleven rows are outside the paired-address frame: one has no land-lot address and ten have a placeholder land-lot number of zero. `sample-kr` uses the remaining 11,993 rows, keeping the smallest 200 seeded SHA-256 hashes of school IDs per authority and numbering class, then up to five distinct streets in each pool. Mountain (`산`) land-lots take precedence over hyphenated road numbers; all others are plain. The draw selects 47 mountain-lot, 80 non-mountain hyphenated-road and 80 plain cases, covers every authority in the snapshot and repeats neither a complete road nor a complete land-lot address. This balances authorities and numbering classes, not population, municipality coverage or address counts; schools and their often larger campuses are a biased frame. Forty-four school locations are held out by the shared location-hash rule.

Every location types both published address strings, followed by `, South Korea`, without the school name or any invented romanization, unit, postcode or region code. Expectations retain the directory's point, country `kr`, a house-level answer and goodMatch true. Either of that row's road or land-lot numbers is an acceptable output number, including the `산` marker when present, through the existing `any_of` format; the two alternatives keep the same point and 250 m tolerance. The tolerance accounts for a school-location point, not a surveyed entrance. Postcodes are not supplied or checked. These are consistent address forms, not damaged queries, and their shared school ID gives both the same location and hold-out assignment. All sampled road and lot numbers differ, so all cases use `any_of`. Alternatives that share a point, country, region and level now also exercise ReverseGeocode at that point; these cases check its country, not the school identity or exact house. ResolveRegion remains unscored here because the directory supplies no ISO region codes. Neither query form tests school-name search or exact entrance accuracy.

The portal's own [DCAT rights metadata](https://www.data.go.kr/biz/dcat/metadata/15021148.do) states `이용허락범위 제한 없음` (no restrictions on reuse), under its [public-data policy](https://www.data.go.kr/ugs/selectPortalPolicyView.do). The corpus records this as `Korea-Public-Data-Unrestricted`, not as a CC0 waiver or an assumed CC licence. The exporter verifies that exact rights statement, uses the same public endpoints as the portal's download button, saves the raw rights metadata, column metadata and pages with checksums, and refuses to overwrite them. A change in the published terms requires review. It needs Python's standard library only and is an offline curation tool, never a runtime download:

```sh
python3 -u tests/registry/export-korean-schools.py --out ../korea-data/korean-schools-2026-10-17.json
target/release/evaluate sample-kr --schools ../korea-data/korean-schools-2026-10-17.json --seed kr-2026-10 --out tests/registry/kr.seeds.jsonl
target/release/evaluate build-gold --seeds tests/registry/kr.seeds.jsonl --out tests/registry/kr.jsonl --schools ../korea-data/korean-schools-2026-10-17.json
target/release/evaluate run --corpus tests/registry/kr.jsonl --index out --out eval-results/kr
```

Retain the snapshot and its `.manifest.json`, `.rights.xml`, `.columns.json` and `.page-N.json` sidecars. The JSON snapshot is 7,467,170 bytes, SHA-256 `984ca7c877b7f3b56af8f61a12e36f1df007d551f349af6b2d75a6b21c20607a`; each case records its filename, checksum, school ID, source reference date, original address strings and rights statement. Rows are sorted by school ID and object keys, so changing response order alone does not change its checksum. An independent bulk-download repeat and the seeded draw were byte-identical. `build-gold --schools` re-reads every sampled ID and rejects duplicate or missing IDs. Coordinates are used directly from the directory and rounded to six decimal places; the corpus generator makes no geocoding request and does not repair source coordinates. Human review of source points is pending; the point's presence and the two matching source address fields do not establish independent real-world accuracy.

On one local full index (builder `83ef195a69f42f93`, Overture 2026-09-23.1 with the local optional sources), SearchAddress passes only 4 of 207 road-name queries and 0 of 207 land-lot queries. Overall there are 4 passes, 173 too-coarse answers, 195 abstentions, 18 near misses and 24 far misses; SuggestAddress passes none of the 414 full strings. The raw index's top answer satisfies the geographic/number checks for 33 queries, ignoring confidence, so the RPC result cannot be attributed entirely to missing index data. This is diagnostic evidence on this school-biased, Hangul-address sample, not a national accuracy rate or a test of romanized Korean addresses.

All 24 critical cases are triaged in `kr.labels.jsonl` after inspecting the original parse, raw index hits and a road-number/country-code control query for each: 13 return the country centroid after losing the location hierarchy, four mistake a house or lot number for an unrelated indexed short postcode, and seven select a same-named place in another region. In one Gyeonggi Gwangju case the raw index has the right house within 60 m of the source point, but the resolver returns metropolitan Gwangju 251 km away. Labels describe these observed parser/matching/fallback failures; no expectation was weakened and no resolver repair was included. A control query returning no house does not prove that the index lacks it. Other failures remain unlabelled, and re-scoring after attaching labels changes no class. The standalone gate passes with the attributed baseline labels, not because the answers pass. A same-binary repeat has zero response drift; a full-suite comparison leaves all 7,945 existing cases' answers unchanged across all four RPCs, with no relative accuracy regression. Both absolute full runs still fail the existing Rue de Rivoli must-pass case, and grpcurl's documented known failure reproduced. These are development observations, not production accuracy or speed claims.

| SearchAddress, land-lot | 0 / 207 | 0 / 207 |
| SuggestAddress, complete road query | 0 / 207 | 56 / 207 |
| SuggestAddress, complete land-lot query | 0 / 207 | 0 / 207 |

Native road/lot numbers retain their hyphens and mountain marker. A numbered answer needs the exact indexed road and number inside the stated administrative hierarchy. A longer road containing the same Hangul bigrams is not the requested road. Conflicting countries, provinces and ambiguous distant houses cannot produce a confident house answer. Suggestions also complete native road and number prefixes. A missing house can answer an explicitly named locality, with `goodMatch` false; an exact street fallback is retained before that locality. No school-directory records or road/lot aliases were imported into the index.

Across the 414 school strings, far misses fall from 24 to 0, abstentions from 195 to 54, and too-coarse answers rise from 158 to 294; near misses fall from 18 to 10, including the new campus miss below. A locality fallback is still a failed house query, not recovered address coverage. There is no demonstrated house-level recovery of land-lot queries on this index.

The same-point alternative check enables the 414 reverse country checks without changing the corpus. A separate diagnostic checks the directory's province names against their established ISO codes: ReverseGeocode and ResolveRegion both pass 388 of 388 points in the 15 mapped provinces. The other 26 queries name the combined 전남광주통합특별시 authority; no ISO code was assumed for it. The checked reverse and region answers are unchanged from the base. These checks do not establish house, school or entrance identity.

The full 8,934-case comparison gains 38 SearchAddress passes (37 school strings and `North Chungcheong, South Korea`) and 56 SuggestAddress passes. Other search answers and all reverse/region answers are unchanged. Unchanged-base and candidate verification runs have zero response drift. Three-request local timing comparisons disagree: one passes the 10% budget, while the final comparison flags SuggestAddress at +11% total, +12% p90/p99 and the unchanged ReverseGeocode at +12% total, +18% p50. The local speed check remains unresolved; these shared-laptop measurements are not evidence of a production speedup.

**The runtime-only relative accuracy gate failed one worse class:** `kr_road-250-e4a66f`, `충청북도 청주시 흥덕구 강내면 태성탑연로 250, South Korea`, changes from abstention to a house-level near miss, 412 m from the directory point with its unchanged 250 m tolerance. The directory identifies 한국교원대학교부설고등학교, while [OSM way 230620326](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/230620326) assigns the same road address to the Korea National University of Education campus. The school point lies inside that campus polygon. The number index retains one point for that address, not the identities or extents of its campus members; an address-only query cannot select the high school. This blocked the runtime-only change; the source-footprint follow-up below removes the unsupported house claim, without a case-specific exception or known-failure label. Coordinates, tolerances, held-out assignments and labels are unchanged.

### Source-footprint follow-up

The builder now distinguishes explicitly tagged OSM buildings (`building` or `building:part`, nonempty and not `no`) from untyped sites. For the latter, a house number is retained only when all outer vertices are available and within 300 m of the first vertex, matching the approximate scale used for merging house copies. This is a conservative import heuristic, not a surveyed accuracy bound. Large or incomplete sites still supply their street, postcode and postal-town metadata; separately addressed nodes and explicitly tagged buildings remain eligible. All outer ways of a multipolygon are checked. The rule is country-independent and does not read school-directory records, case IDs or expected coordinates. Building tags are trusted, compact sites still use their first vertex, and no school or entrance identity is recovered.

The local full rebuild (`a7481f0be4727233`) omitted house numbers on 21,294 untyped ways/relations. Compared against `main` at `69dcf9a`, on the same input snapshots, models and evaluator, all three relative accuracy comparisons pass: builder-only, runtime-only on the rebuilt index, and the combined change. Across 8,939 cases, SearchAddress passes rise from 7,706 to 7,740 of 8,860 scored checks and SuggestAddress from 6,145 to 6,199 of 8,942 checks. No previously passing case fails and no failure class worsens. School SearchAddress passes rise from 21 to 54 of 414; all 54 are road-name queries. Far misses fall from 24 to zero. The campus query now answers the explicitly named locality with `goodMatch=false` and remains a failed house query (`too_coarse`), not a recovered school address.

The builder-only comparison changes two other search points, Tierparkstraße 30 in Munich and Beijing Normal University's address: removing a large site duplicate leaves another indexed point for the same number. Both retain their passing class. Other non-Korean search responses, all suggestions outside Korea, and reverse/region responses are unchanged. Baseline and candidate verification runs have zero response drift, as does the 1,000-address comparison; no accepted snapshot was regenerated. An earlier overbroad draft dropped whole site records and large explicitly tagged buildings, producing a Malaysian house regression and a Thai postcode regression. It was replaced before publication; neither regression remains.

The final combined local speed comparison still exceeds the 10% budget: SearchAddress p90 +11%, SuggestAddress p90 +13% and p99 +33%, and ReverseGeocode p99 +11%. A repeat of the unchanged baseline also exceeds the budget on ReverseGeocode p50 (+13%). This variability does not establish that the change has no slowdown; the new CI speed check remains required. The real-index RPC test still fails its CDG landmark assertion on both base and candidate because this local source set lacks the expected airport landmark. No assertion, corpus expectation, coordinate, tolerance, label, hold-out assignment, model or source snapshot was changed to turn a gate green.

The model-backed synthetic cases check all four endpoints against authored Korean facts. The real-index RPC contract test fails its CDG landmark assertion on both base and head because this local index lacks that landmark; it is not skipped or weakened. grpcurl's documented known failure also reproduces.


## Failure classes

On the local full index with builder `17bc2fab0087eddb` (the source set above plus the OSM extracts), every failing school case was compared with the index itself: `evaluate inspect` lists each street line within 1 km of the school point and every house number stored on it. Held-out schools are not inspected; their classes come from the RPC and raw search only.

| Training cases (163 schools) | Road query | Land-lot query |
|---|---:|---:|
| Requested house indexed within 250 m: passes | 44 | 0 |
| Street indexed within 1 km, requested number not on it | 107 | — |
| No street of that name within 1 km | 12 | — |
| No indexed house within 250 m carries the lot number | — | 163 |

Every training road query whose house the index holds now passes; all other training failures are missing data, answered with the stated locality (too_coarse), a street (near_miss when the road's centroid lies over 2 km away) or nothing. Only 6 of the 163 land-lot queries even have a street named after their 동 or 리 nearby: the index stores no Korean land-lot numbers. The held-out cohort passes 15 of 44 road queries and none of the 44 land-lot ones.

ReverseGeocode's 100% here checks only the country at the school point, not that the index holds the house. The only national source of Korean entrance coordinates, the juso.go.kr location database (also behind the OpenAddresses `kr_*` files), is released per applicant after a purpose review, so the builder does not read it (see `openaddresses_reviewed.tsv`). Pass rates beyond this need a usable address source, not parser or matching work.

The four parse- and fallback-shaped failures that remained were one: the data puts a 리 straight under its 군, outside the 읍 or 면 the address states, so the house was not in that scope. Road names are unique within a 시, 군 or 구, so a house not found under the stated 읍, 면 or 동 is now looked for in the district, for SearchAddress and for a complete road in SuggestAddress. That passes four training and one held-out road query in both RPCs. The full gold comparison on the same index changes no other answer of any RPC. The four labels of now-passing cases are removed, and the other 20 labels now record the failure observed on this index: none is still a country centroid, a far miss or a short-postcode match.
